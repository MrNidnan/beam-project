## What is Beam?

Beam shows the current tango, cortina, and next-tanda information on a second
screen, projector, TV, or any browser on your local network. It reads what your
music player is playing and puts it on screen for the dancers: artist or orchestra,
title, year, the previous song, and more.

It is made for milongas and tango events, but it works anywhere you want a clean,
live "now playing" display. If you can start your music player and use a settings
window, you can use Beam.

## Features

- Works with many popular music players, plus a system-wide **Now Playing** source
- **Moods** that change the look automatically, with timed moods for cues
- Rich **backgrounds**: color, image, slideshow, artist overlays, cover art
- **Profiles** for different venues, DJs, and event styles
- **Browser / network display** for phones, tablets, and side screens
- **Live display controls**: temporary messages and instant blackout
- **Played history**: auto-saved setlist of the night
- A built-in **log viewer** for quick troubleshooting

## Works with your players

Foobar2000, VirtualDJ, JRiver, Mixxx, MediaMonkey, AIMP, iTunes, Spotify, Icecast,
and native macOS/Linux players. The **Now Playing** source follows almost any app
through the operating system's media session — no account or API key needed.

![Player selection in Beam settings](images/user-manual/beam_1_media_selector.png)

See the full support matrix → [Player Support](player-support).

## Moods and timed messages

Moods switch the display style automatically based on the song or playback state.
A timed mood keeps a special layout on screen for a few seconds — perfect for a
`LAST TANDA` cue that returns to normal on its own.

![Mood editor](images/user-manual/beam_mood_editor.png)

More → [Moods and Backgrounds](moods-and-backgrounds).

## Backgrounds and cover art

Use solid colors, single images, or rotating slideshows. Add a per-orchestra
background layer, or use album cover art as the background — with the real aspect
ratio, optional outline, rounded corners, and opacity.

![Artist background with cover art](images/user-manual/beam_artist_background.png)

## Browser and tablet display

Publish the same live screen to any phone, tablet, or browser on your Wi-Fi — with
low latency and several devices at once. Great for a tablet at the DJ table or a
small side display.

![Beam browser display on a tablet](images/user-manual/beam_3_web.png)

More → [Network Display](network-display).

## Live display controls

Instant, manual control of the projected screen during an event: show a centered
temporary message (5–60 seconds) for cues like `BREAK - BACK IN 10 MINUTES`, or
black out the display and resume — without stopping the music, moods, or rotation.

![Temporary message on the display](images/user-manual/beam_mood_temporal_message.png)

More → [Live Display Controls](live-display-controls).

## Profiles

Keep separate, named setups for different venues, DJs, or nights — a formal
milonga, a practica, or a browser-only configuration.

![Profiles screen](images/user-manual/beam_profiles.png)

## Rules and played history

Rules clean up messy metadata: detect cortinas, split combined artist/title text,
ignore unwanted tracks, or trim trailing text from titles. Beam can also auto-save
a setlist of every track you play as text, CSV, and a best-effort M3U8 playlist.

![Rules panel](images/user-manual/beam_rules.png)
